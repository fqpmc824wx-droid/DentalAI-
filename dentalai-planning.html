What is DentalAI?

DentalAI is an AI-powered reception system built specifically for UK dental practices. It will handle inbound calls, prepare appointment requests, capture cancellation/reschedule intent, and recover lost revenue — with Dentally read-only proof first, ElevenLabs voice later, and CallSwitch live routing at the voice pilot stage.

Every booking-changing action is human-approved before it touches the diary. The AI prepares the request, verifies identity/context where available, and creates structured work — then a real person at the practice approves, modifies, or rejects it. No autonomous Dentally writes.

Why Not Autonomous Booking Now?

Direct autonomous AI booking — where software writes directly to the practice diary with no human review — is the direction many competitors are marketing. But for DentalAI, that is deliberately outside the current product model. In UK dental reception, one wrong patient, wrong appointment type, wrong clinician, or wrong diary action can damage trust overnight.

⚠️ The Risk With Autonomous Now
Competitors that sell direct diary writes are taking on avoidable risk. One misread patient record, one double-book, one wrong clinician assignment — and the practice loses trust in the system. DentalAI avoids that by making the approval layer the product, not an afterthought.
✓ Why Rules Change Everything
DentalAI's 69 rules are not just features — they are a governance framework. Every edge case, every risk scenario, every patient type has a defined rule for how the AI behaves. The system can become more automated over time, but the locked rule remains: AI prepares work, humans approve diary-changing actions.

Recovery Engine Principle

Lapsed and recall is not just a reminder tool. It is a controlled work queue that identifies lost or drifting patients, prioritises who should be contacted, creates safe outreach tasks, and prepares booking requests for staff approval. The AI can help call, text, summarise, and prepare options, but it must not confirm bookings or write to Dentally without human approval.

The system should protect patient trust: no spammy outreach, no pressure selling, no uncontrolled campaigns, and no repeated chasing without rules. Every recall action should be explainable, permission-aware, and auditable.

📅 Core Recall & Follow-up Workflows
R-1
Overdue Patient Recall
Identify patients overdue for routine recall and create a staff-approved outreach task. Reception can then call the patient or send the appropriate SMS manually inside Dentally, depending on clinic policy and contact permission.
R-2
Failed-to-Attend Follow-up
When a patient misses an appointment without cancelling, DentalAI flags the slot and initiates a recontact flow within a configurable window.
R-3
Missed Booking Recontact
When a patient calls but cannot be booked in real time, DentalAI logs the intent and triggers a follow-up contact attempt.
R-4
Approval-Pending Notification
Once the AI prepares a recall booking, the patient receives a holding message and staff receive an approval request before the appointment is confirmed.
R-5
Human Approval Gate
No recall booking is confirmed to the patient until a staff member approves it. AI prepares, human confirms.
R-6
Confidence Score on Recall Approval
Staff see a confidence score when approving recall bookings, showing how certain the AI is about the patient match and slot suitability.
R-7
Staff Notification Rules
Reception is notified via app alert and SMS for recall approvals, FTA flags, and recontact outcomes. No notification overload — only actionable alerts.
R-8
Rebooking Flow
When a lapsed patient responds positively, the AI prepares the rebook request using Dentally availability and queues it for staff approval before writing to the diary.
🧩 Lapsed Patient Segmentation
S-1
Overdue Exam Segment
Patients whose last exam date exceeds the clinic recall interval and who have no future exam booked. Used for routine check-up recovery.
S-2
Overdue Hygiene Segment
Patients due for hygiene, private hygiene, or perio maintenance. Kept separate from dentist exam recall so messaging and appointment type stay accurate.
S-3
No Upcoming Appointment Segment
Patients with no future appointment, even if they are not technically overdue. Useful for spotting patients who have dropped out after cancellations or unfinished conversations.
S-4
Treatment Plan Not Booked Segment
Patients with open or proposed treatment and no matching future appointment. Routed as treatment follow-up, not generic recall.
S-5
Private Lead Gone Cold Segment
Private enquiries such as Invisalign, implants, whitening, cosmetic, or consultation leads that were captured but not converted. Requires softer wording and optional TCO/team callback.
S-6
NHS / Private Separation
NHS recall, private recall, and mixed-practice recall should be separated so the AI does not offer the wrong type of appointment or imply availability that the clinic cannot support.
⚖️ Prioritisation & Safety Rules
P-1
Recovery Priority Score
Score recall tasks by months overdue, appointment value, treatment status, FTA risk, debt/account restrictions, private/NHS category, and urgency. Higher priority appears first.
P-2
Debt / FTA Review Gate
If the patient has debt, booking restrictions, or repeated FTA history, the recall task goes to human review instead of normal outreach or booking request preparation.
P-3
Appointment-Type Match
Each recall reason maps to a permitted appointment type, duration, provider group, and diary zone. If the match is unclear, the task becomes human review.
P-4
Contact Permission Check
Before outbound SMS, email, or AI call, the system checks contact permissions, opt-outs, preferred channel, and whether the number/email appears valid.
P-5
Contact Attempt Limits
No pestering. Each campaign has limits such as one call, one SMS, one email, then pause. Repeated failed contact creates a data-quality or human-review task.
P-6
Quiet-Period Outreach Rules
Outbound calls only run in approved clinic windows such as quiet reception periods. No early morning, late evening, Sunday, or bank-holiday outreach unless explicitly configured.
📣 Campaign Governance
C-1
Human-Approved Campaign List
Before any outbound recall campaign starts, staff approve the patient list, campaign reason, wording, channel, and contact window.
C-2
Script Variants
Separate wording for overdue exam, overdue hygiene, FTA follow-up, cancellation rebooking, unfinished treatment, private lead follow-up, and missed-booking recontact.
C-3
No Pressure Selling
Recall messages must be helpful and operational. The AI should not pressure patients, imply clinical urgency without basis, or make misleading claims.
C-4
Bad Number / Bad Email Cleanup
Wrong number, failed SMS, bounced email, or patient says they are not the patient creates a contact-data cleanup task for staff.
C-5
Patient Preference Learning
Store safe preferences such as prefers SMS, morning calls, named clinician, accessibility need, or no AI calls. Use only where policy permits.
🔁 Outcome Tracking & Queues
O-1
Recall Outcome Codes
Every outreach result must be coded: booked request created, declined, no answer, wrong number, moved practice, wants callback, do not contact, human review, or unresolved.
O-2
Unhooked Cancellation SLA
Cancelled or unbooked appointments enter a queue with SLA timers. Same-day gaps and high-value slots should surface faster than low-risk routine recall.
O-3
FTA Escalation Ladder
First FTA creates follow-up. Second FTA flags manager review. Third FTA can trigger booking restrictions depending on clinic policy.
O-4
Inbound Recognition
If a lapsed, unhooked, FTA, or campaign patient calls back, the AI recognises the open recovery context and prepares the correct task instead of treating it as a fresh routine call.
O-5
Approval Before Confirmation
A recall response can create a booking request, but the patient is not told they are booked until staff approve and Dentally write succeeds.
📊 Manager Visibility & ROI
M-1
Recall Queue Health
Managers see stale queues, untouched recalls, unworked FTAs, unhooked cancellations, overdue treatment follow-ups, and clinics falling behind.
M-2
Revenue Recovered Reporting
Track recovered appointments, estimated value, private enquiries recovered, unhooked slots refilled, recall conversion, and FTA follow-up outcomes.
M-3
Staff Workload Reporting
Show how many follow-up tasks AI prepared, how many staff approved, how many were rejected, and where human bottlenecks remain.
M-4
Campaign Comparison
Compare campaign performance by clinic, appointment type, channel, time window, and patient segment to learn what works without over-contacting patients.

Adoption Principle

The staff adoption plan must be explicit: DentalAI is not presented as a replacement for reception. It is presented as a pressure-relief system that catches overflow, prepares work, highlights risk, and gives reception cleaner decisions. Staff remain in command because humans approve diary changes, can listen in during pilot, can take over calls, and can feed back when the AI gets something wrong.

The app should make reception feel more capable at the end of a busy day: fewer ignored voicemails, fewer mystery callbacks, fewer repeated questions, clearer urgent tasks, and less fear that AI is secretly changing the diary behind them.

UX Design Philosophy
How DentalAI should feel for busy reception teams in real clinics
🧭 Product Experience Principles
1
Operational, not decorative
Every screen should help reception act faster.
The interface should feel like a calm control room: dense enough to scan, clear enough to trust, and never dressed up in marketing copy when staff need action.
2
Explain before asking
Staff need to know why an item is present.
Approval cards should show patient context, reason for the action, AI confidence, and the specific rule that triggered the task before asking for a decision.
3
Fast actions, visible control
Trust comes from speed plus an obvious escape hatch.
Approvals, rejections, callbacks, and handoffs should be one-tap actions. Panic override and escalation controls stay visible so the AI never feels autonomous beyond staff control.
4
Queue-first, not page-first
Reception thinks in work waiting, not software modules.
The home screen should start with what needs action now: emergency callbacks, approvals, running-late alerts, lapsed/recontact tasks, and failed writes. Navigation is secondary to the live queue.
5
Show the rule, not the mystery
Every AI item needs a plain-English reason.
A booking request should show why it exists: patient match, detected intent, rule used, confidence score, Dentally verification status, and why human approval is required.
6
Degraded states must be obvious
If Dentally, voice, SMS, or CallSwitch is degraded, staff must know instantly.
Do not hide technical failure. The UI should show human-only mode, Dentally unavailable, SMS delayed, voice degraded, and pending write failure as clear operational states with next actions.
7
Staff dignity by design
The app should never feel like surveillance.
Reports should frame outcomes around workload removed, queues protected, calls recovered, and patients helped. Individual staff metrics should be used for coaching and product feedback, not blame.

🛡️ Core Safety Rules
BR-1
No autonomous diary write in MVP
AI may prepare booking requests, but a human must approve before anything is written to Dentally.
BR-2
Identity before detail
The system must verify patient identity before revealing appointment, balance, family, or treatment-plan information.
BR-3
Emergency always escapes automation
Bleeding, swelling, trauma, severe pain, infection, child urgent, or uncertain emergency wording routes to human escalation.
BR-4
Debt and FTA blocks require review
Booking restrictions, debt flags, and repeated FTA history create human-review tasks rather than automated confirmations.
BR-5
Every action is auditable
Calls, decisions, confidence scores, approvals, rejections, overrides, and diary writes must be logged with user and timestamp.
BR-6
No diagnosis, no treatment advice
Emergency and clinical-sounding calls are routed, not clinically assessed. The AI must not recommend medication, diagnose, or advise treatment.
BR-7
Low confidence means human review
Poor audio, uncertain identity, unclear appointment type, multiple patient matches, or ambiguous rules must create a review task rather than a prepared approval.
BR-8
No sensitive details in external alerts
SMS/email alerts should say a time-sensitive DentalAI task needs review. Patient details stay inside the secure app.
BR-9
No payment details by voice
The AI never collects card details. Payment is handled through approved secure links triggered or approved by staff.
BR-10
No automatic cancellation or reschedule
Cancellation and reschedule intent can be captured, but diary-changing actions require human approval and audit.
BR-11
Dentally unavailable equals degraded mode
If Dentally is unavailable, the AI cannot imply availability or booking. It creates a task and tells the patient the team will confirm.
BR-12
Autonomy is future-only
Autonomous booking is a possible 12–18 month research track after proof, not a current build target or default operating mode.
